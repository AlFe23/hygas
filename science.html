The published methane method

From radiance to a plume-level estimate.

Level-1 retrievals and downstream plume analysis are distinct stages. The published PRISMA, EnMAP, and Tanager-1 paths begin with radiance; EMIT and GHGSat Level-2 products join the workflow with provider-derived enhancement and uncertainty.

  1. 01 · Radiative target

    Represent the gas signal

    A scene-specific lookup table and the sensor spectral response define the radiance signature of a 1 ppm·m methane enhancement.

  2. 02 · Matched filter

    Retrieve enhancement

    Estimate path-integrated enhancement ΔX in ppm·m. Scene, cluster, or detector-column statistics define the selected filter variant.

  3. 03 · Instrument uncertainty

    Propagate radiometric noise

    Use the reference noise model to propagate instrument uncertainty through the filter into a per-pixel σRMN map.

  4. 04 · Scale-aware segmentation

    Review a comparable mask

    Define geometric criteria in physical units, convert them to each sensor's grid, and review connected plume structures against the scene and wind.

  5. 05 · Background uncertainty

    Account for scene clutter

    Select plume-free pixels with continuum spectra similar to the background beneath the mask. Estimate σsurf and combine it with σRMN.

  6. 06 · IME & effective wind

    Estimate mass and flux

    Integrate the enhancement over the reviewed mask, then use the effective-wind formulation to derive flux and propagate the relevant uncertainty terms.

Reading the outputs

Separate instrument noise from background variability.

The retrieval CLI exports ΔX and the instrument-noise term σRMN. For the published Level-1 paths, the downstream notebooks estimate a spectrally matched background-clutter term σsurf and combine the two in quadrature:

σtot = √(σRMN2 + σsurf2)

Here, σtot describes enhancement uncertainty for the plume analysis. IME and flux uncertainty are propagated in the subsequent calculations, including the wind contribution where applicable. The σtot map is produced by downstream analysis; it is not the CLI's *_MF_uncertainty.tif output.

When using the software

Choices worth recording with every result.

Consistency comes from documented inputs and decisions. Keep these with the processing report and the notebook configuration.

Filter statistics and spectral window

Record the matched-filter mode, spectral window, LUT, and reference SNR. CMF, CTMF, and CWCMF use different background statistics; their maps should be interpreted with that choice in view.

Footprint, resolution, and acquisition time

Compare sensors on the shared footprint while retaining their native grids and stated colour scales. Check the acquisition times: source and wind variability also affect a matchup.
